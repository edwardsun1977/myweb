顧問Case Study IV: Subject-Matter Expert Brings More

A financial evaluation system was localized by a team that included a certified public accountant. The translation was accurate — and the accountant's questions turned out to be worth more than the words themselves.

The Challenge

Sometimes the end user seems to come from Mars and the translator from Venus. End users review a translation through the eyes of their profession; translators think from the angle of language. That gap is widest exactly where the terminology matters most — in a specialised field such as accounting.

A linguistically correct rendering can still leave a reviewer uneasy, because the underlying assumptions of the software have not been examined. Nobody in a pure translation workflow is tasked with asking whether the system's logic travels across accounting regimes.

The Solution

We put a CPA on the team. Working through the interface and the documentation, the accountant quickly established that the core of the application rests on international accounting standards. That raised the real questions.

  • As far as we know, the United States remains some distance from accepting principles-based standards — so do US filers working under US GAAP need to reconcile their accounts to IAS or IFRS when they use this system?
  • China's accounting standards are converging with the international framework, but differences remain — income recognition among them. Does the same condition apply when the application is used in China?

Those observations shaped the terminology choices, the help text and the notes we delivered with the build. They also went beyond what the client had asked for.

The Result

The client received more than a translated application: they received a short, practical assessment of where their product's accounting logic meets — and diverges from — the standards its users actually work under.

An expert does not only translate the words. They tell you which words your users will question.
