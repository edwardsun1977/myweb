機械Machinery & Automotive

Machinery translation is a practical subject, and it shows. A sentence can be perfectly literal and still mislead the technician holding the manual — and a misunderstanding on the shop floor is not a stylistic problem, it is a stopped machine, a damaged part, or an injury.

We learned this at the bench rather than in a style guide. Our first serious engagement came with the Caterpillar project: Xiangfan Rail Vehicle Factory, a CAT end user, asked us to revise its Chinese version of a CAT engine manual after the existing translation had left operators guessing. We sat down with the factory, went through the engine systems with the people who service them, and delivered what they actually needed — not a smoother rendering of the English, but instructions a technician could follow.

That work earned the factory's confirmation, and it led to a lasting partnership in which our translations are checked against real machines. The effect on our craft has been decisive: when we translate an engine today, the team knows the engine block in detail — where the oil galleries run, how the cooling circuit is routed, what happens when a torque specification is mistyped.

Our translators also read as engineers. They scrutinise material that has already been translated once, and they raise questions early, long before a small inconsistency becomes an operational hazard. Hence the rule we have worked by ever since.

If you do not understand, you cannot translate.

Safety Is a Translation Issue

Warnings, lock-out steps and torque figures carry legal weight. We keep the original numbering, units and cross-references intact, confirm ambiguous source passages with your engineers instead of guessing, and hold terminology steady across every volume of a manual set. Where the deliverable is a printed book, our multilingual DTP team sets the Chinese edition to publishing standard; where it is an on-board help system, we deliver through technical documentation and software localization.
