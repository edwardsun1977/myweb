市場Marketing & Training

You have already spent the time and the budget preparing your Chinese marketing material. That investment only pays off if the translation is worth reading.

Here is the difficulty. When people scan a brochure, they do not read — they recognise. The page has a fraction of a second to catch the eye before the reader scrolls on. A translation that merely transfers meaning word for word will pass that test only by luck. What you need is copy that is compelling in Chinese on its own terms: idiom, rhythm, headline logic and cultural reference all working to hold the reader long enough to read the paragraph underneath.

That is what we deliver — idiomatic, culturally sensitive Chinese written for the market rather than mapped from the English. Your readers are hooked at first glance, and the description behind the hook does the selling.

Training Material Is a Different Discipline

Training content asks for more than polished language: it usually assumes hands-on knowledge of finance and enterprise management. This is where our background pays off. Course material on controls, reporting, governance and cost management is translated by people who follow those subjects professionally, so a case study keeps its numbers and its logic.

We also contribute articles to the Shanghai National Accounting Institute (ESNAI) website, covering subjects from the Sarbanes-Oxley Act to developments at the Big Four firms. The arrangement lets clients judge our range on a continuous basis — what we can translate, and how we translate it — rather than taking our word for it.

Good marketing translation does not sound translated. It sounds like it was written for the reader in front of it.
