文檔Documentation Localization

With China's accession to the WTO, adapting foreign products to the local language and culture has moved from good practice to regulatory requirement.

A correctly translated user manual does more than satisfy an inspector. It sharply reduces the cost of providing technical support in your target market, and it builds user confidence in your technology. Having spent millions to bring a product into the Chinese marketplace, cutting corners on the language of its documentation is a false economy.

Time and cost are best optimized when localization is built into a project from the start. Bringing the language team in early helps identify the information that must be adapted, keeps adjustments efficient, and reduces the risk of costly re-production. Beyond the obvious savings, that cohesion supports a simultaneous launch in Chinese markets.

So please do it right the first time. We have considerable experience in professional Chinese localization of documentation for companies such as Nokia and Hewlett-Packard.

What We Localize

  • Operating and service manuals
  • Installation, safety and compliance guides
  • Training materials and courseware
  • Packaging, labels and warranty documentation
  • Regulatory submissions and product dossiers

How We Keep It Right

Terminology First

A project glossary is agreed before translation begins, then enforced across every chapter, drawing and label.

Technical Review

Subject-matter experts check the passages where a wrong term would be a safety or compliance issue, not merely an awkward sentence.

Layout and Culture

Screenshots, units, dates, names and examples are reworked for a Chinese reader — not carried over from the English edition.

Print-Ready Output

Final files delivered in your production format, or typeset in house by our DTP team.

A manual is the part of your product the customer reads alone, in their own language, when something has gone wrong. It has to be right.
