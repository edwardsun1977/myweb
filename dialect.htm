方言Which Chinese Do You Need?

Many of our clients find it hard to decide which Chinese to specify. Is the Traditional Chinese used in Hong Kong the same as the version used in Taiwan? Is "Cantonese" a sensible target language? The answers are short, and they save a great deal of rework.

The table below sets out the spoken language and the written script for each of the four markets we most often localize for.

Same Language, Different Scripts

Mainland China and Taiwan both speak Mandarin, yet they write it differently. Mainland China adopted Simplified Chinese — characters with fewer strokes, introduced to make handwriting faster and literacy more widespread — while Taiwan retained Traditional Chinese. The same word is therefore written in two forms, and a text prepared in one is not simply interchangeable with the other.

Language also keeps evolving, and the two written standards have grown apart over the decades. The divergence is especially visible in information technology, where most terminology was coined by different groups of specialists from the 1970s onward: the same function may carry entirely different names on either side of the strait.

Hong Kong Is a Special Case

Hong Kong SAR writes Traditional Chinese, but not quite the Traditional Chinese of Taiwan. Local usage draws on terminology from mainland China, terminology from Taiwan, and a body of terms coined in Hong Kong itself — and the same is true of style and phrasing.

The spoken language of Hong Kong is Cantonese, which is also spoken in Guangdong Province on the mainland. This is why it is incorrect to request Cantonese as a written target language: Cantonese is a spoken variety, and Guangdong Province writes Simplified Chinese rather than the Traditional Chinese used in Hong Kong.

How to Specify Your Target Language

To be certain the project is translated into the right variant, know your target market and always name the language as follows:

  • For the mainland China market: Simplified Chinese for mainland China
  • For the Hong Kong market: Traditional Chinese for Hong Kong
  • For the Taiwan market: Traditional Chinese for Taiwan
  • For the Singapore market: Simplified Chinese for Singapore

If you are unsure, tell us where the product will be sold and who will read it. We will recommend the variant, and confirm the terminology set that goes with it.
