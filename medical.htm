醫療Medical & Pharmaceutical

Medical translation has a vocabulary of its own, and it punishes literalism. A term carried across word for word may be grammatically flawless and clinically wrong — and in this field the consequences are measured in patient safety, regulatory acceptance and liability, not in reader satisfaction.

Clear, correct and consistent information is therefore only the baseline. High-risk content also demands several levels of quality control and a working knowledge of stringent regulatory requirements. We apply a methodology built for that: multiple rounds of proofreading and editing by native-speaking reviewers, each with a defined scope, so that terminology, dosage conventions and cross-references are checked more than once and by more than one pair of eyes.

Where a document carries legal weight — consent, liability, data protection — we add an expert with a legal background to complement our medical reviewers. The clinical meaning and the legal intent of the original are then preserved together, which is what a submission or an ethics committee actually examines.

In medicine, a translation that reads well but means something slightly different is a defect, not a style choice.

Built for Review, Not Just for Delivery

Regulated content is read by regulators, ethics boards and clinicians who will query anything that looks inconsistent. We therefore maintain a project glossary from the first file and hold to it across every subsequent document, so a term used in a protocol reads the same way in the CRF, the patient leaflet and the label. Layout is handled with the same care: for printed labelling and patient leaflets our multilingual DTP team sets the Chinese edition to submission standard, while electronic materials flow through document translation.
