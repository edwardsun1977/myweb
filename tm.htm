案例Translating at the Speed of Your Business

Case study: Nokia IP VPN Gateway — how translation memory let us deliver 30,000 words a week without sacrificing consistency.

The Challenge

It is a fast-paced world out there. When Nokia developed their IP VPN gateway, they needed its manuals translated truly "as soon as possible" — at a sustained rate of 30,000 words a week, no small feat. Further, as general IT equipment, its popularity rested on constantly evolving requests and on-going content updates. It was therefore vital that the translation overcome the technical challenges of later updates as well as the linguistic consistency on which the manuals depended.

The Solution

We used a translation-memory database created specifically from our past Microsoft and McAfee projects. The memory provided a common basis on which translations of general network equipment could rest, allowing us to re-use existing experience with the OSI seven-layer model, the IPSec protocol, network infrastructure and much more.

In addition, fuzzy matching allowed translation segments that were similar — but not identical — to the source text to be inserted and then edited. This let us deliver a final, flawless translation in days. More importantly, the use of translation memory minimized the daunting task of managing continuous changes, which would otherwise very likely have spiraled.

Translation memory is not just about speed — it is about carrying consistency forward through every update.
